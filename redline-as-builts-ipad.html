Redline as-built drawings on an iPad

The as-built is only as good as what makes it back to the office. Here's a field workflow that keeps the redlines on the drawing as you go, instead of in a notebook, and gets a clean PDF to the PM at the end.

Before you start

  • Get the current set on the iPad. Open the PDF from Files, or share it straight out of Mail. It works offline, so do this while you still have a signal.
  • Set your initials. Stamps carry your initials and the date, and so do the markups the office sees.
  • Keep the toolbar simple. Switch off the tools you don't use, or pick Simple markup for the crew.

1. Find the sheet

Scroll the set top to bottom, or jump straight to a sheet. Pinch to zoom in on the room you're standing in.

2. Cloud the change

Drag a revision cloud around anything that isn't built the way it's drawn. Add a text note next to it: "Moved 4' east per GC", "Homerun to LP-2, not LP-1". Short and specific beats long. If the drawing is wrong underneath, use white-out in white, gray or any color, then draw what's really there.

3. Draw it with real symbols

Place the device that actually went in from 600+ trade symbols in 18 libraries. Size a symbol once and keep placing it across the sheet. Use the pen for conduit routes and the highlighter to mark what's done. Everything you place can be counted later, the same way you'd count devices on a PDF.

4. Measure what moved

Pick Measure and Rough-In reads the scale off the sheet's title block. Tap Use it, then measure in feet and inches: a distance between two points, or a run you tap out point by point. It reads notes like 1/4" = 1'-0" and 1" = 20'. If the sheet says NTS, calibrate off a dimension you know.

5. Pin a photo where it matters

Drop a photo pin right where you took the picture: the junction box above the ceiling, the sleeve through the wall, the panel before the cover went on. Photos travel with the drawing and get their own pages in the flattened PDF.

6. Stamp it AS-BUILT

When the sheet is done, stamp it AS-BUILT. The stamp adds your initials and the date. There are others for the in-between: FIELD VERIFIED, REVISED, NOT IN SCOPE, HOLD and APPROVED. If the office needs a signature, draw yours once and sign with a tap.

7. Send the office a PDF it can use

Use Share to send what the office needs:

  • PDF for Bluebeam or Acrobat. Markups stay editable, with your initials, so the PM can work from them. See A Bluebeam alternative for electricians.
  • Flattened PDF. Markups burned in, with photo and count pages. Good for the owner's closeout package.
  • Marked-up sheets only. Skip the 180 sheets nobody touched.
  • .redline file. The whole drawing with every markup, count and photo, to open on the office PC and keep working.

When a new revision comes out

Don't redo the redlines by hand. Open the new revision and compare it to the old print. Removed work shows red, new work blue, and you can bring your old markups over to the new sheets. See Compare two revisions.

Questions foremen ask

Do I need internet in the field?
No. Rough-In works offline with no account. Drawings, markups and photos stay on the iPad until you send them.
Will the office be able to edit my redlines?
Yes, if you send the PDF for Bluebeam / Acrobat. The markups arrive as editable comments with your initials and the date.
Does it work on an iPhone too?
Yes. The same app runs on iPad and iPhone, and on a Mac with Apple silicon. One Rough-In Pro purchase covers them with the same Apple Account.
What does it cost?
Every tool is free for your first 3 drawings. Rough-In Pro is $39.99 once for unlimited drawings, with no subscription.
