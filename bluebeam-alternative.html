Guide · Field and office

A Bluebeam alternative for electricians

Bluebeam Revu is the standard in a lot of offices, and it earns that spot. But a foreman redlining an iPad in a basement doesn't need all of it. Here's an honest look at when each tool fits, and how they work together.

The short answer

If your work is estimating, takeoff and coordinating big sets with a lot of people at once, stay with Bluebeam. If you need a crew to redline prints, count devices and measure runs on an iPad or a trailer laptop, and hand the office a PDF it can keep editing in Bluebeam, that's what Rough-In Redlines was built for. Plenty of shops will use both.

When Bluebeam is the right tool

  • Estimating and takeoff. Bluebeam has area and volume measurements, takeoff tools and markup reports built for estimators. Rough-In counts devices and measures lengths and runs, but it has no area tool and it isn't an estimating program.
  • Studio Sessions. When several people need to mark up the same file at the same time, Bluebeam's Studio does that. Rough-In has no cloud and no accounts. Files move by email, OneDrive, Teams or AirDrop.
  • Big office workflows. If your PMs live in the Markups list, batch work across big sets, or your GC sends Studio invites, Bluebeam is the tool everyone already speaks.
  • Your company has already standardized on it. Don't fight that. Make the field side easier instead.

When Rough-In fits

  • Field crews on an iPad or iPhone. Apple Pencil, finger or gloves, with big targets. Settings can cut the toolbar down to Simple markup for crews who only need a pen, clouds and text.
  • A one-time price. Every tool is free for your first 3 drawings. Rough-In Pro is $39.99 once, with no subscription. On Windows, one license key covers 2 PCs. Bluebeam is sold as a subscription; see Bluebeam's pricing page for their current plans.
  • No signal, no account. Rough-In works offline, there's no sign-up, and drawings stay on your device.
  • Trade tools out of the box. 600+ symbols in 18 trade libraries, Find & Count, measuring in feet and inches with the scale read off the title block, compare revisions, signatures, stamps and real redaction. Panel schedule cards are on the iPad.
  • The same app in the office. It runs on iPad, iPhone, Mac with Apple silicon, and Windows 10 and 11. A .redline file carries a drawing and every markup between the iPad and the PC.

Working with an office that runs Bluebeam

This is the part that matters most. Nobody in the office should have to retype a foreman's notes.

  • PDF for Bluebeam. Export a PDF where the markups stay editable comments in Bluebeam or Acrobat, with author, subject and date. The PM can move, reply to or delete them like any other markup.
  • Live measurements (Windows). From the Windows app, distances and runs arrive as Bluebeam Length measurements with the sheet's scale, and count marks arrive as Count measurements, so they show up with values in Bluebeam's Markups list.
  • Bring your tool chest (Windows). Import a Bluebeam Tool Chest (.btx) into My Palettes so the crew uses the same symbols the office does.
  • Everyone else. A flattened PDF with the markups burned in, plus photo and count pages, opens anywhere. The counts also go out as a CSV for the estimator.

What Rough-In doesn't do

Better to know now than after you've bought it:

  • No area or volume takeoff, and no estimating.
  • No live multi-user sessions and no cloud storage.
  • No Android app.
  • Panel schedules are iPad only for now. Inserting Word and Excel files, and exporting password-protected PDFs, are Windows only.

Try it on a real job

The fairest test is one of your own sets. Open a real print, cloud a change, count the receptacles and send the PDF to whoever runs Bluebeam in your office. If it opens editable on their end, you'll know. The free version has every tool for 3 drawings, and the built-in sample drawing doesn't count against them. Questions foremen ask

Can Bluebeam edit markups made in Rough-In?
Yes. Export the PDF for Bluebeam / Acrobat and the markups arrive as editable comments with author, subject and date. From the Windows app, measurements and counts also arrive as Bluebeam Length and Count measurements.
Is Rough-In a subscription?
No. Every tool is free for your first 3 drawings. Rough-In Pro is $39.99 once for unlimited drawings. On Windows, a license key covers 2 PCs.
Does it work without internet?
Yes. Rough-In works offline, needs no account, and keeps your drawings on your iPad, iPhone, Mac or PC.
Is Rough-In affiliated with Bluebeam?
No. Rough-In Redlines is made by RK Technology LLC in North Dakota and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Bluebeam, Inc.
