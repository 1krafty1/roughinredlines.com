A field-first alternative to Bluebeam for the trades

Rough-In Redlines is PDF markup for electricians, foremen and small trade contractors. Redline the prints, place trade symbols, count devices and measure on the iPad, iPhone, Mac, Windows PC or Android tablet, with no signal needed. Then send the office a PDF they can keep editing in Bluebeam. Free for 3 drawings; Pro is $39.99 once.

Bluebeam Revu is a desktop PDF markup tool that a lot of estimators, design teams and offices use every day. This page isn't about replacing it there. It's for the people who mark up prints on the job: in a basement, in a trailer, on a tablet with gloves on. If that's you, here's what Rough-In is and whether it fits.

Who it's for

  • Foremen and journeymen who redline, count and measure, and need to send it back the same day.
  • Small electrical and trade shops that want every crew lead marking up prints without a monthly bill per person.
  • Electricians, plumbers, HVAC, fire alarm and low-voltage crews: the symbol libraries cover the trades, not just one.
  • PMs who sign, redact, combine and print PDFs, and need the field's changes back in a form the office can use.
  • Estimators who want quick device counts and runs in a spreadsheet.

Works with the PDFs you already have

Rough-In opens the drawing sets you already get: PDFs from Files or straight out of Mail, photos and pictures, and password-protected PDFs.

  • PDFs marked up in Bluebeam open too. The markups already in them show on the sheet as they were drawn, and Find & Count searches the sheet as you see it, so it can count symbols that were placed in Bluebeam.
  • Your markups stay editable for the office. Export the PDF for Bluebeam / Acrobat and every cloud, note and symbol arrives as an editable markup with author, subject and date. Counts arrive as Bluebeam count measurements with their totals, and measurements at the sheet's scale.
  • The round trip works. When that PDF comes back from the PM, your markups come back as editable markups, and the markups the PM added show on the sheet.
  • Same symbols as the office. On Windows, import your Bluebeam tool chest (.btx) into My Palettes.

When to keep what you have

Rough-In marks up PDFs and pictures. It doesn't draw or edit CAD files, it isn't a plan room with RFIs, submittals and transmittals, and it doesn't run estimating takeoffs with cost databases and bid reports. If your work depends on those, keep the tool you have, and let the crews use Rough-In alongside it. The PDF markup apps comparison has the full, honest fit.

Questions people ask

Is Rough-In a replacement for Bluebeam?
For the work most crews do on a print, yes: redlines, trade symbols, counts, measuring, compare, sign and print. It isn't trying to be a full office suite. If your work is detailed estimating with cost databases, or a plan room with RFIs and submittals, keep the tool you have. Many shops use both: Rough-In in the field, Bluebeam in the office.
Does Rough-In work on iPad?
Yes. Rough-In Redlines is on the App Store for iPad and iPhone, with Apple Pencil, finger or gloves. The same download runs on Macs with Apple silicon. It's also on Windows 10 and 11 and on Android tablets.
Can my PM open my markups in Bluebeam?
Yes. Choose Send PDF, then PDF for Bluebeam / Acrobat. Your clouds, text, symbols and other markups arrive as editable markups in Bluebeam Revu and Adobe Acrobat, with author, subject and date, and counts arrive as Bluebeam count measurements with their totals.
Can I open PDFs that were marked up in Bluebeam?
Yes. Any PDF opens, and markups already in it show on the sheet as they were drawn. Find & Count searches the sheet as you see it, so it can count symbols that were placed in Bluebeam. When a PDF you sent from Rough-In comes back from the PM, your markups come back as editable markups, and the markups the PM added show on the sheet.
Can I use my Bluebeam tool chest?
Yes, on Windows. Import a Bluebeam tool chest (.btx) into My Palettes and each stamp in it becomes a symbol, so the crew uses the same symbols as the office.
Is Rough-In a subscription?
No. Every tool is free for your first 3 drawings. Rough-In Pro is $39.99 once for unlimited drawings: a license key for 2 PCs on Windows, or a one-time in-app purchase on the App Store. On Android tablets, Pro is a one-time purchase through Google Play. The only monthly plan is Crew, the team version, at $15 per person per month, available now and optional.
Is Rough-In affiliated with Bluebeam?
No. Rough-In Redlines is made by RK Technology LLC in North Dakota and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Bluebeam, Inc.
