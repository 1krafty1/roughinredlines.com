Electrical drawing markup app for electricians

An electrical drawing markup app lets you redline the PDF, place real electrical symbols, count devices and measure runs, then send it back. Rough-In Redlines does that on Windows today, built for electricians and the other MEP trades.

What an electrician needs from a markup app

General PDF editors are made for contracts and forms. A set of E sheets needs different tools. Here's the checklist we built Rough-In Redlines around, and how it handles each one.

  • Real electrical symbols. 600+ symbols in 18 trade libraries, for electrical and the other trades. Capture a symbol off the drawing, or import a Bluebeam tool chest (.btx) on Windows, so the crew uses the same symbols the office does.
  • Device counts. Box one receptacle and Find & Count marks the rest, with totals by sheet and for the set.
  • Measuring to scale. The scale is read off the sheet, and you measure runs in feet and inches.
  • Compare revisions. Removed work shows red, new work blue, with a list of every change, including text like “20A → 30A”.
  • An export the office can use. A PDF whose markups stay editable in Bluebeam and Acrobat, a flattened PDF, a counts CSV, or a print up to 36×48 on the plotter.
  • Works with no signal. Every tool works offline in a basement or a mechanical room, and your drawings stay on your device.
  • A price a foreman can approve. Free for 3 drawings. Pro is $39.99 once, with no subscription.

A typical markup, start to finish

  1. Open the set and find your sheets. Open the PDF, then jump straight to the E sheets.
  2. Set up your symbols. Pick the electrical library, or import your shop's Bluebeam tool chest (.btx) into My Palettes.
  3. Mark the changes. Cloud each change, add a note and place the right device.
  4. Count and measure. Find & Count the receptacles and fixtures, and measure the runs.
  5. Send it. Export a PDF for Bluebeam / Acrobat and a counts CSV for the estimator.

Not just electrical: MEP and low voltage

The same tools work for plumbers, HVAC, fire alarm and low-voltage crews. Switch the toolbar to Simple for people who only need a pen, clouds and text, or leave every tool on for the foreman. The toolbar can sit on any side of the window.

What it doesn't do

Rough-In marks up PDFs and pictures. It isn't CAD, so it doesn't draw or edit DWG files, and it isn't estimating software with a cost database. For how it sits next to other apps, see PDF markup apps for electricians, compared.

Questions people ask

Does it have electrical symbols?
Yes. There are 600+ trade symbols in 18 libraries, for electrical and the other trades. You can also capture a symbol off the drawing, or import a Bluebeam tool chest (.btx) on Windows.
Can plumbers and HVAC crews use it?
Yes. The symbols and tools are built for the trades, not only electricians: plumbers, HVAC, fire alarm and low-voltage crews use the same markup, count and measure tools.
Can it make panel schedules?
Yes, on the iPad today, and on Windows in the next update. You get an Excel file and a PDF directory card with your company name and logo.
Can it open DWG or CAD files?
No. Rough-In marks up PDFs and pictures. On Windows you can also insert Word and Excel files as PDF pages.
